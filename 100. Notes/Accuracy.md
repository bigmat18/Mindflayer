# Accuracy

How close is human perceptual judgement to some objective measurement of the stimulus? studied in psychophysics, the branch of psychology devoted to measuring human perception

**The power law of Stevens** (1975) models our response to sensory experience:
![[Pasted image 20251215185652.png | 350]]

The power law exponent n ranges from the sublinear 0.5 for brightness to the superlinear 3.5 for electric current. Sublinear phenomena are compressed, superlinear phenomena are magnified
- e.g., doubling the physical brightness results in perception that is considerably less than twice as bright

![[Pasted image 20251215185727.png | 250]]

Length (of a line segment on a 2D plane perpendicular to the observer) has an exponent equal to 1
- our perception of length is a very close match to the true value
![[Pasted image 20251215185833.png | 300]]

- the other visual channels are not perceived as accurately (e.g., area is compressed)
![[Pasted image 20251215185911.png | 300]]

Other answers come from controlled experiments that directly map human response to visually-encoded abstract information, and provide explicit rankings of perceptual accuracy for each channel type
- e.g., Cleveland and McGill 1984 and subsequent updates
